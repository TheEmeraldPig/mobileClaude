Wiring

XIAO seen from the top, USB-C at the top
Battery + → switch middle pin. Switch outer pin → B+ pad.
Battery − → B− pad. Every other ground goes to the GND pin.
D0 → 220 Ω resistor → LED long leg. LED short leg → GND.
D1 → button. Button's other leg → GND.

Use 30 AWG silicone wire. Pin names match the firmware in glasses/firmware/lens_glasses. Check Seeed's pinout picture before soldering: the battery pads are on the underside of the XIAO.

Settings

  • PETG is best (handles a hot car); PLA is fine for a first try.
  • 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 25% infill.
  • No supports, no brim (add a brim to the temples if they lift).
  • About 45 g of filament, 4–5 hours in total.

Make it fit you

  • Measure the width of your head just in front of your ears and set --head-width (default 132).
  • Measure your board stack with the camera unplugged; if it's over 11.6 mm, set --board-thickness.
  • Then run python3 frame.py in glasses/hardware to make new STL files.

Build order

about an evening

  1. Flash and test on the desk first

    Upload the firmware with the board loose on the desk (see the glasses README) and check it reaches the hub. It's much easier to fix anything before it's glued in.

  2. Test-fit the board and battery

    Drop the board into the front of the right temple, USB-C down into the slot in the floor, then the battery behind it. Sand or regenerate with new sizes if anything is tight.

  3. Wire the power

    Solder battery + to the switch's middle pin, the switch's outer pin to B+, and battery − to B−. Glue the switch inside the floor slot so its lever sticks out underneath. Insulate every joint with heat-shrink.

  4. Fit the camera

    Swap in the long-ribbon camera on the Sense board. Feed the camera head first through the opening at the front of the temple into the pocket at the back of the right end piece, lens forward, so it shows through the round window. A dot of glue holds it.

  5. Fit the light and button

    Push the LED into the small hole below the camera from behind and wire it with its resistor to D0 and GND; the wires run back into the temple through the same opening. Glue the button inside the round hole on the outer face of the temple and wire it to D1 and GND.

  6. Join the temples to the front

    Cut four 11 mm pieces of filament. Push two into the holes on the back of each end piece, add a little superglue to the joint faces, and press each temple on. Hold for a minute.

  7. Close up

    Stick the antenna flat on the inside of the right lid, plug it into the board, then press the lid on and fit the M2 screw at the back. The left lid can hold a few coins under it if the glasses tip to the right.

  8. Switch on

    Slide the switch on: the light blinks twice when the glasses are on the hotspot. Charge through the USB-C port underneath with the switch on.

Battery safety

Use a cell with a protection circuit, never squash or puncture it, keep it away from the soldering iron, and stay nearby for the first few charges. If it swells or gets hot, unplug it and take it out.
